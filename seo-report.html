    The short verdict

    The product is good, the formula is genuinely differentiated and the brand voice is strong. The problem is not the product — it is that the site never tells a search engine what it sells.     01 · Executive Summary

    The situation in five sentences

    1. 01
      The site is effectively closed to search.
      The homepage title is 18 characters with no keywords, the H1 tag is empty, and there is no Organization, WebSite, FAQPage or BreadcrumbList schema anywhere. Nothing on the site answers the question “what is sold here?”
    2. 02
      No keyword-targeted page architecture was ever built.
      Only two collections exist. There is no landing page for “electrolyte powder”, “electrolyte sachets” or “hydration” — terms drawing thousands of UK searches a month. Veloforte's single electrolyte collection carries 1,500+ words of copy and a seven-question FAQ.
    3. 03
      The biggest opportunity is magnesium, not electrolytes.
      In the UK, “magnesium” draws roughly three times the search interest of “electrolytes”, and “magnesium glycinate” alone is about half of it. Selene's headline ingredient is magnesium bisglycinate — the formula already earns the right to that demand — yet no page targets those terms.
    4. 04
      81 of the 97 blog posts are trend journalism with no commercial value.
      Labubu toys, the Met Gala, the Yo-Chi chain. They carry no search volume, they do not connect to the product, and none of them has a subheading — which rules them out of featured snippets and AI answers. Three posts are live under one identical title.
    5. 05
      “Best electrolytes UK” cannot be won with a product page.
      Publishers own that result page: Good Housekeeping, Women's Health, Glamour, the Telegraph, the Independent, GQ and the Evening Standard. The only route in is digital PR — seeding product and getting into those lists. Balance appears in none of them.

    What people actually search for

    • best electrolytes — the most-searched related query. People are looking for a decision, not a brand.
    • what is / what are electrolytes — a very large audience is still searching for the basic definition. Category education is the first touchpoint before purchase.
    • electrolytes uk — the “UK” modifier is strong. People want domestic manufacture and fast delivery. “Made in the UK” is our advantage and appears in no title tag.
    • electrolytes boots — retail intent. People are searching for it in-store.
    • electrolytes tablets ≈ electrolytes powder — tablet demand matches powder demand, and we have no tablet product.
    • urea and electrolytes, electrolytes blood test — medical intent. Do not target these; they do not convert and they carry YMYL risk.

    The quick-win cluster

    • magnesium bisglycinate for sleep — rising 100%, Selene's headline ingredient, not one word about it on the site.
    • electrolytes for sleep — too niche for Trends to measure, but almost nobody competes; we could define the category.
    • zero sugar electrolyte powder — rising 140%, and Selene is stevia-sweetened.
    • l-glycine benefits, glycine for sleep — we carry 1,250 mg of L-glycine and never say so.
    • chamomile / passionflower benefits — the formula's other two actives; posts exist but carry no subheadings and no link to the product.
    • uk made electrolyte powder — low volume, very high conversion, zero competition.

    The real competitor

    Publishers

    There is not a single brand site in the top 10 for “best electrolytes uk” or “best electrolytes for women uk”. Good Housekeeping, Women's Health, Glamour, the Telegraph, the Independent, GQ, the Evening Standard and affiliate sites hold it.

    Our answer: that result page is not entered with content, it is entered with digital PR — product seeding and editor relationships.

    Seven things competitors do that we do not

    • Put the specification in the title. Love Life Supplements: Ultimate Electrolytes Powder (500mg Sodium + Potassium) — UK Made. Ours: Selene Electrolytes – Balance.
    • Keyword URLs. lytes.uk → /collections/electrolyte-drinks. Us → /collections/products.
    • FAQs and long copy on the collection page. Veloforte writes 1,500+ words under “What Is Electrolyte Powder?”, “How Much Should You Take?”, “When Should You Take?”. Our collection pages carry no copy at all.
    • A named scientific authority. Phizz names Dr Paul Anastasiades with his title and his university. Our site names no nutritionist, dietitian or other qualified expert.
    • A free tool. Precision's Sweat Test is both a link magnet and an email-list engine.
    • Flavour breadth. Humantra's nine flavours are nine separate long-tail search clusters.
    • Review volume. 5,000 reviews drive conversion, rich results and citation in AI answers alike.

    05 · Technical & On-Page Audit

    What is missing and what is wrong

    28 findings in order of impact. Each carries evidence — a real value pulled live from the site — and a concrete fix. Every critical one can be resolved from the Shopify admin or the theme code.

    CRITICAL8 findings

    01  ·  The homepage title tag contains no keyword at all

    The title tag is the single strongest on-page signal of what a site sells. “balance” alone names no product, no category and no market — and it is an extremely generic word competing against millions of pages.

    <title>balance. – Balance</title>   — 18 chars of a 60-char field
    FixWrite a 55–60 character title carrying category, differentiator and market: Electrolyte Sachets Made in the UK | Magnesium & Sleep Support — Balance

    02  ·  The homepage H1 tag is completely empty

    An H1 element exists but renders empty. To a search engine that reads as “this page has no main subject.” It is also an accessibility defect for screen-reader users.

    document.querySelectorAll("h1") → 1 element, textContent: ""
    FixAdd a visible, keyword-bearing H1 to the hero section.

    03  ·  The product URL tells search engines nothing

    “Selene” is an internal product name; nobody in the UK searches for it. URL, title and H1 all omit the category — so the site's only electrolyte product carries no strong signal matching the word “electrolyte”.

    https://beinbalance.uk/products/selene
    FixChange it to /products/selene-electrolyte-powder-magnesium and 301-redirect the old path.

    04  ·  There are no keyword-targeted collection pages

    In ecommerce, generic category searches are won by collection pages, not product pages. There is no landing page for “electrolyte powder” (~3,000 searches/month), “electrolyte sachets” or “hydration”. Veloforte ranks on exactly this architecture.

    All collections in sitemap: /collections/products · /collections/bundle-and-save
    FixCreate four collections — /collections/electrolyte-powder, /collections/electrolyte-sachets, /collections/hydration, /collections/magnesium-supplements — each with 800–1,200 words of original copy and an FAQ.

    05  ·  No Organization, WebSite or LocalBusiness schema anywhere

    These schemas let the brand be recognised as an entity in Google's knowledge graph. Without them the brand is an anonymous website — and assistants such as ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini have nothing to recognise or recommend.

    Homepage JSON-LD: Organization ✗ · WebSite ✗ · SearchAction ✗ · BreadcrumbList ✗ · LocalBusiness ✗
    FixAdd Organization (logo, sameAs, address, contact) + WebSite + SearchAction JSON-LD to theme.liquid, plus LocalBusiness for the Manchester address.

    06  ·  Not one of the 97 blog posts has a single subheading

    Every post is one H1 followed by an unbroken block of text. Three consequences: no chance at featured snippets or People Also Ask; AI models cannot extract passages, so they do not cite it; and low readability shortens time on page.

    12 posts sampled and re-verified in a rendered browser — H2/H3 inside article body: 0
    FixMake H2/H3 mandatory in the article template. Retrofit question-form H2s into the 20 highest-potential posts first.

    07  ·  Two collection pages serve identical content

    Textbook duplicate content and split authority. Google cannot decide which to rank, so neither performs.

    /collections/all → H1 “Products”, self-canonical
/collections/products → H1 “Products”, self-canonical
Both indexed; /collections/all is absent from the sitemap yet appears in search.
    FixPoint the canonical of /collections/all at /collections/products, or 301-redirect it.

    08  ·  Three posts are live under one identical title

    Identically titled pages cannibalise one another and drag down the site's overall quality signal. The slug/content mismatch also points to a break in the publishing process.

    /blogs/articles/the-mind-book-club
/blogs/articles/the-mind-book-club-1
/blogs/articles/the-mind-book-club-2
All three <title>: “The 'Mind' Book Club – Balance”
Also: /dopamine-menus-…-viral-1 actually contains “Hot Yoga vs Reformer Pilates”.
    FixConsolidate the three “Book Club” posts into one series page and 301 the others. Re-slug the mismatched post to match its content.
    HIGH14 findings

    09  ·  Seven FAQs on the product page, no FAQPage schema

    Visible FAQs: “What Are Electrolytes?”, “How Do I Take Them?”, “Do they taste salty?”, “Can Selene Help with PMS Symptoms?” … → JSON-LD FAQPage: absent
    FixWrap the existing FAQs in FAQPage JSON-LD — more SERP real estate and a shot at AI answers, with zero new content written.

    10  ·  All blog meta descriptions are auto-generated and cut at 320 characters

    H1 count = 0: /pages/about-us · /pages/our-store · /pages/store-location · /pages/sale · /pages/coming-soon · /pages/resources · /pages/events
    FixPromote the first section heading from H2 to H1 in the template. “Born in Manchester” is already a good H1.

    12  ·  Eight pages have a completely empty meta description

    descLen = 0: /collections/bundle-and-save · /blogs/articles · /pages/about-us · /pages/our-store · /pages/store-location · /pages/sale · /pages/coming-soon · /collections/all
    FixWrite a 150-character description for each; put price, benefit and market signals into the collection ones.

    13  ·  One page's meta description is broken

    The template has picked up the page's button labels as its description. Shown in a search result, it makes the brand look unfinished.

    /pages/resources → meta description: "View Now ✕ View Now ✕ View Now ✕"
    FixWrite one by hand, or set the page to noindex.

    14  ·  The product meta description runs 320 characters and ends mid-sentence

    "…Made in the UK " ← the sentence stops here (320 chars)
    FixReplace with 150–155 characters carrying the dose and the differentiator: 20 sachets · 135mg magnesium bisglycinate, chamomile & L-glycine. UK-made electrolytes for hydration and better sleep.

    15  ·  379 requests and 87 JavaScript files on a single page

    Loaded apps include Klaviyo, Tidio, Judge.me, Appstle, UpPromote, Froonze, Globo Form Builder, Secomapp Affiliate, Vimeo, jQuery, ionicons, DOMPurify, Mustache, two separate Google Tag Manager IDs and the Facebook Pixel. That payload hits interaction latency (INP) directly and costs mobile conversion.

    Product page, mobile viewport: 379 resource requests · 87 script requests · 56 external <script src> · 3,422 DOM nodes · 14+ third-party domains
    FixAudit the app stack: remove what is unused, collapse the two GTM IDs into one, bundle libraries like jQuery and ionicons into the theme, and lazy-load the video and chat widgets on interaction.

    16  ·  The social share image is served over an insecure protocol

    og:image → http://beinbalance.uk/cdn/shop/files/front_pouch3.png
    FixSwitch it to https://. Some platforms refuse to render http images at all.

    17  ·  The product has 11 reviews

    Review volume drives conversion, the credibility of the aggregateRating rich result, and whether AI assistants cite you at all.

    Judge.me → 11 reviews · Humantra → 5,000+ (4.9★) · Phizz → 4,000+
    FixAutomate post-purchase review emails after delivery, incentivise photo reviews, and prompt again at subscription renewal. Target: 250+ reviews.

    18  ·  The blog has no category or tag structure

    With 97 uncategorised posts, neither a reader nor Google can build a mental map of what this site is an authority on.

    /blogs/articles → 97 posts in one flat list; a category block exists in the template but is empty
    FixCreate five categories — Hydration & Electrolytes, Sleep & Recovery, Women's Health, Nutrition, Culture — each with 300+ words of intro copy.

    19  ·  The product page carries only ~897 visible words

    Product page visible words: 897 · Veloforte collection page: 1,500+
    FixTake it past 1,800: what each ingredient does, a milligram table, a competitor comparison table, a “who it's for / when to take it” section and an expanded FAQ.

    20  ·  Thin and empty pages are left indexable

    No H1 and no original copy: /pages/coming-soon · /pages/sale · /pages/store-location · /pages/wishlist · /pages/recently-viewed-products · /pages/discount5 · /pages/giveaway · /pages/giveaway-register
    FixSet noindex,follow on the ones with a user function, delete the empty ones, and drop both from the sitemap.

    21  ·  One product, one flavour

    Each flavour is its own long-tail search cluster. A single flavour caps both the searchable surface and repeat-purchase variety.

    Selene → “Summer Berry” only · Humantra → 9 flavours
    FixPut 2–3 flavours on the roadmap, each with its own variant URL and copy.

    22  ·  No E-E-A-T signals — the most dangerous gap in a supplements category

    Supplements sit inside Google's “Your Money or Your Life” category, where ranking without visible expertise, experience, authoritativeness and trust is close to impossible. Phizz names its science lead with title and university.

    Nowhere on the site: nutritionist or dietitian name and credentials · company address · author profiles · scientific references
    FixName the consulting nutritionist with their accreditation (AfN/BDA registration) on a dedicated expert page, have them review the product page and every health-related article with a visible “Reviewed by” line, add an author box to every post, cite sources under health claims, and show the full company address and number.
    MEDIUM6 findings

    23  ·  No BreadcrumbList schema on any page

    FixAdd it to the product and collection templates — the SERP then shows a navigation path instead of a raw URL, which lifts click-through.

    24  ·  The product page carries two separate Product schemas

    JSON-LD blocks: ["Product", "Product"] — most likely the theme and an app both emitting one
    FixRemove one — conflicting schemas can make Google discard the rich result entirely.

    25  ·  The Manchester presence is doing no work in search

    FixOpen or optimise a Google Business Profile, add LocalBusiness schema, and build a real local page for “electrolytes Manchester” and “wellness events Manchester”.

    26  ·  Brand-name collision: “Balance Journal”

    A publication trading under the same name and publishing in your category muddies your brand searches.

    balancejournal.co ranks in your exact category with “Best Electrolytes UK”, “Best Electrolyte Powder UK” and “Electrolyte Statistics UK 2026”
    FixAlways position the name alongside the product (“Balance Selene Electrolytes”) and disambiguate the entity with brand schema and sameAs links.

    27  ·  No hreflang tags

    link[rel=alternate][hreflang] → 0
    FixNot a problem today with a single market — but if Ireland or the EU is on the roadmap, build the architecture before you need it.

    28  ·  Almost no internal linking from blog to product

    You already have posts on magnesium, L-glycine, chamomile and passionflower — all four are Selene ingredients. None links to the product page in context.

    FixTwo in-copy, keyword-bearing links from each ingredient post to the product, and links back from the product page to the posts.

    • Canonical tags are correct and consistent on every page.
    • robots.txt is clean; filter and sort parameters are properly blocked.

    08 · Method & Sources

    How this report was produced

    Method

    • Technical audit: 20 pages of beinbalance.uk were fetched live and their HTML parsed — title, meta, canonical, heading hierarchy, JSON-LD schema, image alt text, internal and external links, resource requests and DOM count.
    • Demand data: United Kingdom Google Trends data pulled live for this report — five-year time series, top and rising related queries, regional spread and category comparisons.
    • Volume estimates: Trends reports relative index only. Terms were compared on one scale and anchored to a reference of 40–60k/month for “electrolytes” (UK); results are presented as ranges.
    • SERP analysis: UK search results were reviewed for five head terms, via DuckDuckGo UK because Google could not be queried directly.
    • Competitive analysis: seven brands' sites were reviewed against the same criteria; review and distribution figures come from the brands' own pages and the trade press.

    Limits

    • The backlink profile could not be measured — it requires a paid tool.
    • Core Web Vitals field data was unavailable; the speed findings rest on live in-browser measurement and resource counts.
    • Search volumes are estimates, not measurements.
    • Blog findings were verified on a sample of 12 of the 97 posts.
    • Market size figures were excluded because they could not be independently verified.
